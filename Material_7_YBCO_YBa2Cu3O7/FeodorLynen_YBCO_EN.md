Research Proposal (Exposé)

1 Research question and scientific significance

Memristive devices based on the valence change mechanism switch their resistance through field-driven redistribution of oxygen defects \[1–3\], with the oxygen-defect formation energy entering all quantitative models exponentially \[3, 4\]. Energy research has established the metal–oxygen covalency as the controlling descriptor of this energy \[5–8\] – but the experimental test of this link in memristive devices is missing. My project performs this test on YBa2Cu3O7-δ (YBCO): the one oxide outside the perovskite manganite/ferrite families in which all three evidence pillars of a clean descriptor study already coexist.

\(i\) Covalency vs. stoichiometry is measured with unmatched precision: the site-specific, polarisation-resolved O-1s/Cu-2p XAS of YBCO resolves the doped-hole count separately for the CuO2 planes, the CuO chains and the apical oxygen as a function of oxygen content δ and cation doping \[9, 10\] – a level of spectroscopic resolution no perovskite series offers. The chain-oxygen content δ is continuously and reversibly tunable between 6.0 and 7.0 by annealing, making it an exceptionally fine stoichiometry axis. (ii) EV vs. site and stoichiometry is computed: DFT point-defect studies give site-resolved vacancy formation energies (chain ≈ 1.2 eV vs. plane ≈ 2.1 eV in YBa2Cu3O6.5) that depend on the oxygen content itself \[14, 15\]. (iii) Switching at noble interfaces is established and replicated: Au/YBCO/Au cells switch bipolar and non-volatile via interfacial oxygen-vacancy migration \[11\], with recent replications and mechanistic extensions from 80 to 300 K \[12, 13\]. The gap: all published devices are ceramic; no epitaxial, δ-controlled, statistically robust device series with simultaneous XAS covalency quantification exists. My central hypothesis: the switching figures of merit of Au/YBCO devices scale with the chain-hole covalency measured by site-resolved XAS – making YBCO the sharpest single-material test of the descriptor chain and, at the same time, the first extension of the planned “treasure map” beyond the perovskite structure family.

Design constraints are addressed openly: below Tc ≈ 93 K superconductivity takes over (all device metrology therefore runs at ≥ 100 K and room temperature), and the ortho-ordering kinetics of the chain oxygen add a slow structural relaxation that is monitored by XRD. Both are charted boundaries, not confounders of the room-temperature descriptor study.

Conceptual footing (sharpened after expert feedback, 08/2026): throughout this project, “covalency” is used in the quantitative sense of the charge-transfer/cluster-model framework – charge-transfer energy Δ, on-site Coulomb energy U and transfer integrals, in the Zaanen–Sawatzky–Allen classification \[22\] and as extracted compound by compound by the configuration-interaction analyses of Fujimori, Bocquet, Saitoh, Sarma and co-workers \[23–26\]. O-K pre-edge intensities serve as a relative, series-internal hybridisation measure only; their limits as an absolute covalency scale (core-hole effects, ligand-hole distribution, band effects) are treated explicitly \[27\], and the conflation of hybridisation with covalency that is common in the catalysis-descriptor literature is deliberately avoided – the descriptor scale of this project is calibrated against cluster-model analysis, not against pre-edge intensity alone.

2 Why the Chueh group at Stanford is the ideal place

The group of Prof. William Chueh is a world leader in exactly the combination this project requires: defect chemistry and non-stoichiometry of redox-active oxides, quantitative operando X-ray absorption spectroscopy at the in-house Stanford Synchrotron Radiation Lightsource (SSRL), and redox chemistry of epitaxial oxide thin films \[18–20\]. The group’s seminal work on the redox activity of oxygen anions in oxygen-deficient perovskites \[19\] is a direct methodological blueprint for my project; the Nature Materials study on atomically defined LaNiO3 surfaces \[20\] – with Christoph Baeumer from the Jülich school as first author – furthermore demonstrates that the group routinely masters epitaxial oxide model systems of the type proposed here and successfully integrates German postdocs. Direct SSRL access makes XAS campaigns plannable that in Europe cost months of lead time through competitive single proposals. Complementarily, I contribute the device side, from which the Stanford environment profits in turn: PLD process development, memristive device processing and statistically robust switching characterisation from five years at the Peter Grünberg Institute (PGI-7, group of Prof. Dittmann) \[E1–E3\]. The constellation is thus a genuine two-way transfer of knowledge. For complementary epitaxial growth questions, the environment of the Stanford Institute for Materials and Energy Sciences (SIMES) at SLAC is available. As a Bessel Research Award winner (2021), Prof. Chueh is a member of the Humboldt Network and has repeatedly hosted German postdocs – the host eligibility required by the programme is given.

3 Work programme (24 months)

**WP1 – Synthesis and descriptor measurement (months 1–10)**

PLD growth of epitaxial YBCO films on SrTiO3 and NdGaO3 in the Stanford/SLAC thin-film laboratories; δ-series (six oxygen contents between O6.4 and O7.0) set by controlled p(O2) annealing, verified by c-axis XRD and transport; structural validation (XRD/RSM, AFM). In parallel, first XAS campaign at SSRL: site-resolved O-K pre-edge quantification (plane/chain/apex following \[9, 10\]) and Cu-L2,3 valence analysis for every δ → experimental covalency scale of the series. Milestone M1 (month 10): validated δ-series with complete site-resolved descriptor set.

**WP2 – Devices and switching characterisation (months 8–18)**

Au-active pads (replicating the proven noble-contact geometry \[11–13\]) and Pt pads as second inert flavour, processed photolithographically with ≥ 30 nominally identical cells per δ; quasistatic and pulsed characterisation at 100–300 K under the statistics protocol established in my doctorate (ON/OFF, forming requirement, kinetics, retention, endurance). Milestone M2 (month 18): switching figures of merit as a function of δ, in epitaxial cells, with robust statistics – the first non-ceramic Au/YBCO device series.

**WP3 – Correlation, operando validation and publication (months 16–24)**

Correlation analysis site-resolved covalency (XAS) ↔ site-resolved EV (DFT \[14, 15\]) ↔ switching metrics; test of the chain-hole hypothesis. Second SSRL campaign: XAS on switched cells (ex-situ before/after, operando if successful) to verify δ-dependent VO dynamics at the Au interface. Publication of the first covalency-resolved cuprate memristor study; dataset released following FAIR principles. Milestone M3 (month 24): submitted publication(s), public dataset.

5 Career perspective and return strategy

The project is the deliberate building block between my doctorate (memristive devices, PLD, switching statistics) and my longer-term goal of establishing my own junior research group in Germany on descriptor-based materials development for memristive oxides (Emmy Noether proposal in preparation; ERC Starting Grant as an alternative). The stay with Prof. Chueh closes the one competence gap of my profile – quantitative synchrotron XAS and defect thermodynamics – and at the same time delivers the empirical foundation for the larger programme of a “treasure map” of memristive oxides in analogy to the bonding-descriptor maps of Wuttig et al. \[21\]. The choice of YBCO is deliberately map-strategic: it adds the cuprate charge-transfer bonding class and a second structure family to the descriptor map, testing whether the covalency–EV–switching correlation generalises beyond the perovskites – the decisive question for the map’s predictive claim. The return is concretely planned: reintegration into the German oxide-electronics environment (FZ Jülich/RWTH Aachen, JARA), supported by the Feodor Lynen Return Fellowship, with submission of the group proposal during the final year of the stay. The Stanford/SSRL collaboration built up abroad will be continued as the transatlantic bridge of the future group.
